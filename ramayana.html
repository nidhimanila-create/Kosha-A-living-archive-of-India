The Epic

A story that travels.

The Ramayana is one of the great narrative traditions of South Asia. At its centre is Rama, whose life is shaped by questions of duty, loyalty, sacrifice and kingship.

The story follows Rama's exile from Ayodhya, his years in the forest, the abduction of Sita, the alliance with Hanuman and Sugriva, the journey towards Lanka and the eventual return to Ayodhya.

Across centuries, the story has been retold through literature, performance, temple traditions, painting, sculpture, dance and regional storytelling. Different communities and regions have developed their own versions, emphases and interpretations.

One story. Many retellings. A cultural memory still alive.
Walk Through the Story

From Ayodhya to Lanka.

Follow the broad narrative journey of the epic through four defining movements.

I

Ayodhya & Exile

Rama's life as heir to Ayodhya changes when a promise made by Dasharatha leads to his fourteen-year exile. Sita and Lakshmana choose to accompany him into the forest.

II

The Forest Years

Rama, Sita and Lakshmana travel through forests and hermitage landscapes. Their story becomes intertwined with encounters involving Surpanakha, Ravana and Jatayu, eventually leading to Sita's abduction.

III

Alliance & Search

Rama meets Hanuman and Sugriva and forms an alliance with the vanara forces. The search for Sita leads the story southward towards the ocean and Lanka.

IV

Lanka & Return

Rama's forces reach Lanka. The conflict with Ravana culminates in Rama's victory, followed by the return towards Ayodhya and the beginning of a new chapter.

Reading the Past Carefully

Tradition is not the same as proof.

Kosha distinguishes between literary tradition, religious belief, archaeological evidence and historically documented places. A location being associated with the Ramayana for generations is culturally significant, but that association does not automatically establish that the epic events occurred there as literal historical events. This distinction allows the story to be explored with both cultural respect and historical care.
